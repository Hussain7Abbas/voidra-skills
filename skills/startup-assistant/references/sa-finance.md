Lead on cash, budgets, unit economics, financing scenarios, pricing, and packaging. Evaluate affordability, buyer value, and the cost of delivering the promise together. Do not carry SaaS targets into services, retail, hardware, or marketplaces without adaptation.

## Financial analysis

Establish currency, period, tax treatment where material, actuals versus forecasts, restricted versus usable cash, and accounting basis. Request the missing decisive inputs rather than guessing. Label illustrative numbers and keep them out of factual company records.

| Metric | Definition and important limit |
| --- | --- |
| Net cash burn | Cash outflows minus cash inflows for the same period; identify financing flows separately |
| Simple runway | Usable cash / positive monthly net cash burn; a constant-rate estimate only |
| Gross margin | (Revenue minus cost of revenue) / revenue; define included costs and handle zero revenue |
| Unit contribution | Net unit revenue minus the variable costs to serve/deliver that unit; disclose discounts, fees, refunds, support and logistics assumptions |
| Break-even units | Fixed period costs / positive unit contribution for a sufficiently stable sales mix and capacity |
| CAC | Defined acquisition spend / newly acquired customers in a compatible period/cohort; distinguish paid-channel from fully loaded CAC |
| CAC payback months | Acquisition cost per customer / monthly contribution from that customer under a stated cost definition; show churn and timing limits |
| Simplified LTV | Monthly contribution / monthly customer churn only under a stable, homogeneous subscription model; not reliable with zero churn, immature cohorts, or inconsistent definitions |

When cash timing matters, use a dated cash schedule with collections, payroll, taxes, inventory, payables, debt, and committed spend. Positive accounting profit or prepaid annual revenue does not guarantee future liquidity. If net burn is zero or negative, simple runway is not an infinite forecast: show downside liquidity instead.

Use base and downside assumptions; add upside only when useful. Vary the drivers that can change the decision: retention, conversion, collections, price, demand, gross margin, hiring ramp, or supplier cost. Avoid aggregate averages that hide loss-making segments. Separate a projected improvement from a measured result.

For hiring or vendors, include fully loaded cost, setup, ramp, minimum commitments, cancellation, working capital, and operational capacity. Finance advice is analytical support; identify specific tax, accounting, securities, or financing issues requiring qualified local review when they affect the action.

## Pricing and packaging

Treat three decisions separately: the charging unit, what is included, and the amount charged. Evaluate buyer value and alternatives, budget, delivery economics, comprehensibility, predictability, and incentives. A competitor's price is evidence of its offer, not proof that the same price fits this business.

Use real willingness-to-pay evidence where available. Interviews and surveys inform hypotheses; transactions, paid pilots, renewal behavior, and documented win/loss outcomes test them more directly. Initial pricing can be a bounded learning proposal without pretending to be statistically optimized. Avoid universal price buckets or mandatory three-tier packaging.

For a proposed increase, assess new and existing customers separately, contractual notice, price sensitivity, discounts, churn, support load, and communication. Model contribution and retention, not just the price multiplier. For a price rise from 20 to 30, holding recognized revenue constant would require retaining two-thirds of the original paying volume; that arithmetic is not a churn prediction or profit forecast.

Deliver the recommended structure or spend decision, explicit calculations, sensitivity, rollout/test, and stop condition. Use the marketing perspective for communication and sales/product for buyer behavior and entitlement consequences.

Formula reference: [SBA break-even calculator](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/break-even-point-calculator/). Check applicability to the business rather than treating a single-product model as universal.
